Step-by-Step Interpretation of Chest X-ray & Chest CT

Dr. Maher Ghazawi · 26 questions · Quiz 1, Mon 5 Oct

High-yield card read first

The 4 Ds

  • Detect (silent, systematic, hidden areas, periphery)
  • Describe (quality, correct terms, location, pertinent negatives)
  • DDx (categorise by the dominant abnormality)
  • Decision (clinical, previous imaging, labs, further imaging)

Image quality (PIRP)

  • Penetration: good = thoracic vertebrae visible through the heart; under = vertebrae/diaphragm obscured; over = lungs too dark
  • Inspiration: 9–10 posterior or 5–6 anterior ribs
  • Rotation: spinous processes midway between the medial clavicle ends
  • Position: PA = true heart size; AP magnifies the heart

Normal anatomy

  • Trachea central; carina angle 40–80°
  • Heart: 2/3 left, 1/3 right; CTR < 50% (PA)
  • Right hemidiaphragm higher (≤ 2.5 cm) or equal; both sharp
  • Costophrenic angles sharp; left hilum higher than right; vessels taper to periphery
  • Lateral view: 4 lucent areas (retrosternal, retrocardiac, retrotracheal, spine)

Terms & signs

  • Silhouette sign localises disease (lost border = adjacent lobe)
  • Consolidation hides vessels (air bronchograms); ground-glass = hazy ↑ attenuation with preserved vessels/bronchi
  • Nodule ≤ 3 cm, mass > 3 cm
  • Cavity DDx CAVITY: Cancer, Autoimmune, Vascular, Infection (TB), Trauma, Youth (congenital)
  • CT pulmonary angiogram: filling defect = PE

Memory hooks

4 Ds

Detect, Describe, Differential, Decide.

PIRP

Penetration, Inspiration, Rotation, Position.

CAVITY

Cancer, Autoimmune (GPA), Vascular (septic emboli/infarct), Infection (TB, abscess), Trauma, Youth (congenital).

"Right is higher, Left hilum is higher"

Right hemidiaphragm higher; left hilum higher.

Exam traps

Don't call cardiomegaly on an AP film; AP magnifies the heart.
The left hemidiaphragm is normally lower, and the left hilum higher. Reversed = abnormal (volume loss, mass).
Ground-glass keeps vessels visible; consolidation hides them.
Mediastinum pushed away = tension pneumothorax / large effusion; pulled toward = collapse.
A normal CXR does not exclude PE; CT pulmonary angiography does.

Q1Past-paper style

How do you determine good penetration on a CXR?

Answer C: Contours of the lower thoracic vertebrae visible through the heart shadow

Good penetration = see through the heart to the thoracic spine. Ribs count = inspiration; clavicle symmetry = rotation.

Source: Slide 'Assess image quality – penetration'; 2023 Final (Radiology Q3)

Q2Past-paper style

Which statement about the normal CXR is correct?

Answer A: Normal carina angle is 40–80°

Carina 40–80°; the right hemidiaphragm and left hilum are higher.

Source: Slide 'Normal CXR'; 2023 Final (Radiology Q5)

Q3Past-paper style

Increased lung attenuation on CT with preserved bronchovascular markings?

Answer B: Ground-glass opacity

GGO is hazy attenuation through which vessels and bronchi remain visible; consolidation obscures them.

Source: Slide 'Consolidation vs GGO'; 2023 Final (Radiology Q6)

Q4Past-paper style

What is the purpose of the silhouette sign?

Answer E: Localising the disease

Loss of a normal border tells you which lobe is involved (e.g. right heart border → RML).

Source: Slide 'Silhouette sign – to localise'; 2023 Final (Radiology Q7)

Q5Past-paper style

CT shows a cavitating lesion. Most likely cause (past-paper key)?

Answer D: TB

Infection (TB, abscess) is a leading cavity cause; also cancer (squamous), GPA, septic emboli (CAVITY).

Source: Slide 'Cavitation – CAVITY'; 2023 Final (Radiology Q4)

Q6Past-paper style

Hypertensive patient; CXR shows an enlarged cardiac shadow. Most likely?

Answer C: Cardiomegaly from LV hypertrophy / hypertensive heart disease

Long-standing hypertension → LVH and eventually dilated LV. Always check the view first: AP films exaggerate heart size.

Trap: Recall was uncertain (no picture); remember the AP-magnification trap.

Source: 2025-26 Quiz 1 (Radiology)

Q7Past-paper style

A patient presents with fever and a productive cough. His PA chest X-ray is shown. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Answer E: Right middle lobe consolidation (pneumonia)

There is ill-defined airspace opacity in the right mid/lower zone without a meniscus or volume loss. With fever and sputum, this is lobar consolidation (pneumonia). An effusion would blunt the costophrenic angle with a meniscus, and a pneumothorax would be lucent with no lung markings.

Source: 2025-26 Final 1 Paper 1 Radiology Q1 (recall)

Q8Past-paper style

A young man has sudden left-sided pleuritic chest pain and dyspnoea. His chest X-ray is shown. What is the diagnosis?

Answer D: Left pneumothorax

A visceral pleural line runs parallel to the left chest wall, with no lung markings beyond it. That is a pneumothorax. A large bulla can mimic it, but a bulla has a concave inner margin and usually some markings across it.

Source: 2025-26 Final 1 Paper 1 Radiology Q2 (recall)

Q9Past-paper style

A chest CT (lung window) like the one shown demonstrates hazy increased attenuation of the lung parenchyma through which the vessels and bronchial walls are still visible. What is this finding called?

Answer A: Ground-glass opacity

Ground-glass opacity is increased attenuation that still lets the bronchovascular markings show through. Consolidation hides them. A cavity is a gas-filled space with a wall. The recaller said the exam CT was similar to this one but not the same; this image also shows reticulation and honeycomb cysts at the bases.

Source: 2025-26 Final 1 Paper 1 Radiology Q3 (recall)

Q10Past-paper style

A patient has sudden dyspnoea and pleuritic chest pain. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest (pulmonary arterial phase) is shown. What is the diagnosis?

Answer B: Pulmonary embolism

On a CT pulmonary angiogram, a PE is an intraluminal filling defect within a contrast-filled pulmonary artery. Aortic dissection would show an intimal flap in the aorta, not a pulmonary arterial defect.

Source: 2025-26 Final 1 Paper 1 Radiology Q4 (recall)

Q11Past-paper style

A 30-year-old has dyspnoea and chest pain after a car accident. The left side is hyper-resonant with reduced tactile fremitus, and the trachea is shifted to the right. What will the chest X-ray show on the left?

Answer E: Homogeneous jet-black hypertranslucency without lung markings

Pneumothorax: hyperlucent ('jet black') hemithorax with no lung markings and a visible lung edge; mediastinal shift away = tension. A meniscus opacity is pleural effusion.

Source: IM 2023 file – Pulmonology Dr Bassiony recall Q2; Part 1 Final pulmonary recall

Q12Past-paper style

A contrast-enhanced chest CT is shown. What is the diagnosis?

Answer B: Pulmonary embolism

A filling defect (low-density clot) inside the contrast-filled pulmonary artery = PE. Aortic dissection shows an intimal flap in the aorta.

Source: IM 2023 file – Part 1 Final recall (Radiology, image 3)

Q13High-yield

Adequate inspiration on a PA CXR is shown by?

Answer C: 9–10 posterior ribs above the diaphragm

9–10 posterior (or 5–6 anterior) ribs.

Q14High-yield

How is rotation assessed?

Answer D: Spinous processes midway between the medial ends of the clavicles

In a non-rotated film the spinous processes project centrally between the clavicle heads.

Q15High-yield

Which view gives the true heart size?

Answer A: PA view

In PA the heart is close to the detector → minimal magnification. AP exaggerates heart size.

Source: Slide 'Position – PA real heart size'

Q16High-yield

Loss of the right heart border on PA CXR localises disease to?

Answer D: Right middle lobe

The RML abuts the right heart border; the lingula abuts the left heart border.

Source: Slide 'Silhouette sign' (standard)

Q17High-yield

Normal relationship of the hemidiaphragms?

Answer B: Right higher by up to 2.5 cm (or equal)

Right up to 2.5 cm higher (liver) or both level; both should be sharp.

Source: Slide 'Diaphragmatic cupola'

Q18High-yield

Upper limit of normal cardiothoracic ratio on a PA film?

Answer A: 50%

Heart < 50% of thoracic width on PA.

Source: Slide 'Normal CXR – heart less than 50%'

Q19High-yield

Which is NOT one of the 4 Ds?

Answer A: Dose

Detect, Describe, DDx, Decide.

Q20High-yield

On CT pulmonary angiography, PE appears as?

Answer C: Intraluminal filling defect in a pulmonary artery

Contrast outlines a clot as a dark defect within the opacified artery.

Q21CBL / AMBOSS style

A stabbed patient is hypotensive; CXR shows no lung markings on the right, a depressed right hemidiaphragm, widened intercostal spaces and the mediastinum shifted left. Diagnosis?

Answer E: Tension pneumothorax

Air under tension pushes everything away. Collapse pulls the mediastinum toward the lesion.

Source: CBL2-W1 Q4 image

Q22CBL / AMBOSS style

A 52-year-old with restrictive PFTs and clubbing has a CT showing clustered subpleural cystic air spaces at the bases. Name the pattern.

Answer C: Honeycombing

Honeycombing = end-stage fibrosis (UIP/IPF pattern).

Source: CBL1-W1 Q3 image

Q23CBL / AMBOSS style

A smoker with dullness and reduced breath sounds at the left base. Which CXR sign is expected?

Answer A: Blunting of the costophrenic angle

Pleural fluid collects in the costophrenic recess (meniscus sign). Deep sulcus sign = pneumothorax on a supine film.

Source: CBL2-W1 Q3

Q24High-yield

On a supine ICU film, an abnormally deep, lucent costophrenic angle on one side suggests?

Answer E: Pneumothorax (deep sulcus sign)

Air rises anteriorly and inferiorly in a supine patient, deepening the costophrenic sulcus.

Source: Standard CXR sign (supine pneumothorax)

Q25Exam trap

Portable AP chest film in ICU shows a large heart. Best interpretation?

Answer D: Heart size can't be reliably judged on an AP film

AP magnifies the heart; confirm on PA or echo.

Source: Slide 'AP view – exaggerated heart size'

Q26Exam trap

Lung collapse (atelectasis) of the left lower lobe: which way does the mediastinum move?

Answer B: Toward the collapse (left)

Volume loss pulls structures toward it; effusion/tension pneumothorax push them away.

Source: Pleura slide 'Lung collapse' + CXR 'Describe' (standard)
