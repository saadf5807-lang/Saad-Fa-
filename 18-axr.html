Step-by-Step Interpretation of the Abdominal X-ray

Dr. Maher Ghazawi · 21 questions · Quiz 1, Mon 5 Oct
High-yieldTables & flowchartsMemory hooksTrapsPractice

High-yield card read first

Views & technique

  • Acute abdominal series: supine AP + erect (erect CXR for free air)
  • Can't stand → left lateral decubitus (free air collects over the liver)
  • Inclusion: liver, spleen, lateral abdominal walls, pubic symphysis
  • Good exposure: the spine is clearly visible
  • Describe location using the 9 abdominal regions

ABCDE detection

  • Air in the wrong place: pneumoperitoneum, pneumoretroperitoneum, pneumobilia, portal venous gas
  • Bowel: dilated or not; small vs large; cause (hernia, volvulus, stone)
  • Calcification: gallstones, renal/ureteric/bladder stones, pancreatic, adrenal, vascular
  • Disability: bones (fractures, lesions, spine), solid-organ enlargement
  • Everything else: clips, catheters, drains, IUCD, lung bases

Free air

  • Erect CXR: air under the hemidiaphragm = perforation
  • Supine: Rigler sign (both sides of bowel wall visible), football sign, falciform ligament
  • Pneumobilia: air in bile ducts (post-ERCP/sphincterotomy, gallstone ileus)
  • Portal venous gas (periphery of liver): ischaemic bowel — ominous

Small vs large bowel (3-6-9)

  • Small bowel: central, valvulae conniventes cross the full width, dilated > 3 cm
  • Large bowel: peripheral, haustra don't cross fully, dilated > 6 cm (caecum > 9 cm)
  • Obstruction: dilated loops + air–fluid levels on erect; collapsed distal bowel
  • Sigmoid volvulus: coffee-bean loop from the pelvis
Lecture vs textbook · Most AXR slides are images; the 3-6-9 rule, Rigler sign, coffee-bean sign and decubitus side are standard radiology facts added to explain the images.

Memory hooks

ABCDE

Air, Bowel, Calcification, Disability (bones/organs), Everything else.

3-6-9

Small bowel > 3 cm, colon > 6 cm, caecum > 9 cm = dilated.

"Left side down for air"

Left lateral decubitus: free gas rises over the liver, away from the gastric bubble.

DDDD

Detect, Describe, DDx, Decision (same as chest).

Exam traps

Free air is best seen on an erect chest X-ray (under the diaphragm), not on a supine AXR.
Most gallstones are not visible on plain film; ultrasound is the test.
Valvulae conniventes cross the whole lumen; haustra don't. Don't swap them.
Always check the lung bases and bones on an AXR (Everything else / Disability).

Q1Past-paper style

What indicates free peritoneal gas?

Answer B: Free air under the hemidiaphragm

Pneumoperitoneum on an erect film collects under the diaphragm (perforated viscus).

Source: 2023 Final (Radiology Q1)

Q2Past-paper style

Usual abdominal X-rays taken for an acute abdomen?

Answer A: Supine and erect AP

Acute abdominal series: supine + erect (plus erect chest). Unstable → left lateral decubitus.

Source: Slide 'Basic views (acute abdominal series)'; 2023 Final (Radiology Q2)

Q3Past-paper style

Radiograph shows pneumoperitoneum after sudden severe abdominal pain. Diagnosis category?

Answer D: Gastrointestinal perforation

Free gas = hollow viscus perforation (e.g. perforated ulcer).

Source: Recall list B (Radiology: GI perforation)

Q4Past-paper style

Radiograph shows multiple dilated central loops with air–fluid levels. Diagnosis?

Answer C: Small bowel obstruction (ileus)

Dilated small bowel (valvulae conniventes, central) with air–fluid levels = obstruction/ileus.

Source: Recall list B (Radiology: ileus); F1P2 key (Q19 intestinal obstruction)

Q5Past-paper style

A middle-aged woman has recurrent right upper quadrant pain after fatty meals. Her abdominal X-ray is shown. What is the abnormality?

Answer E: Gallstones

There is a cluster of faceted, ring-like calcified opacities in the right upper quadrant, typical of gallstones ('C – Calcification' on the slide). Pancreatic calcification lies across the epigastrium, and a renal stone lies over the renal outline beside the spine. Most gallstones are radiolucent, so ultrasound remains the test of choice.

Source: 2025-26 Final 1 Paper 1 Radiology Q5 (recall)

What does the "A" in the ABCDE approach stand for?

Answer B: Air in the wrong place

Pneumoperitoneum, pneumoretroperitoneum, pneumobilia, portal venous gas.

Source: Slide 'ABCDE'

Q7High-yield

Which view is used when the patient cannot stand to look for free air?

Answer A: Left lateral decubitus

Left side down: free gas rises over the liver where it is easy to see.

Source: Slide 'Left lateral decubitus'

Q8High-yield

Which structures must be included for an adequate AXR?

Answer D: Liver, spleen, lateral abdominal walls and pubic symphysis

Inclusion check on the technique slide.

Source: Slide 'Normal (Technique) – inclusion'

Q9High-yield

Sign of adequate exposure on an AXR?

Answer C: The spine is clearly visible

Per slide: the spine should be clearly visualised.

Source: Slide 'Exposure'

Q10High-yield

Mucosal folds crossing the full width of a dilated loop identify?

Answer E: Small bowel (valvulae conniventes)

Valvulae conniventes cross completely; haustra only partly.

Source: Slide 'B – Bowel' (standard)

Q11High-yield

Colon is considered dilated above?

Answer A: 6 cm

3-6-9 rule: small bowel 3, colon 6, caecum 9 cm.

Source: Standard rule (bowel slide)

Q12High-yield

Air within the biliary tree on AXR is called?

Answer B: Pneumobilia

Seen after sphincterotomy/ERCP or with gallstone ileus; central in the liver (portal venous gas is peripheral).

Source: Slide 'Air in wrong place'

Q13High-yield

Calcification across the epigastrium on AXR suggests?

Answer A: Chronic pancreatitis

Pancreatic calcification = chronic pancreatitis.

Source: Slide 'C – Calcification'

Q14High-yield

Which item belongs to the "E – Everything else" check?

Answer C: Surgical clips, catheters, drains, IUCD, lung bases

Lines, devices and the lung bases are easy to miss.

Source: Slide 'E – Everything else'

Q15High-yield

A 63-year-old with 4 days of crampy diffuse abdominal pain, vomiting and no bowel movement; AXR shows markedly dilated colon with a large bent loop arising from the pelvis ("coffee bean"). Diagnosis?

Answer D: Sigmoid volvulus

Sigmoid volvulus: coffee-bean sign from the pelvis.

Source: Slide 'Bowel – cause (volvulus)'

Q16CBL / AMBOSS style

After ERCP, an AXR shows branching air centrally in the liver. Most likely?

Answer E: Pneumobilia (expected after sphincterotomy)

Central branching gas = biliary; peripheral branching gas reaching the capsule = portal venous (ominous).

Source: Slide 'Pneumobilia / portal vein gas'

Q17Exam trap

Best plain film to detect a small pneumoperitoneum?

Answer D: Erect chest X-ray

As little as 1–2 mL of free gas can be seen under the diaphragm on an erect CXR.

Source: Standard teaching (acute abdominal series)

Q18Exam trap

Why is a normal AXR unhelpful for suspected gallstones?

Answer B: Most gallstones are radiolucent; use ultrasound

Only a minority are calcified; ultrasound is first-line.

Source: Slide 'Calcification – gallstone' (standard)

Q19Exam trap

Where should portal venous gas be seen, compared with pneumobilia?

Answer E: Peripheral liver, reaching the capsule

Portal flow carries gas to the periphery; bile flows centrally toward the hilum.

Source: Slide 'Pneumobilia vs portal vein gas'

Q20High-yield

Seeing both the inner and outer walls of bowel loops on a supine AXR (Rigler sign) indicates?

Answer A: Pneumoperitoneum

Free intraperitoneal gas outlines the outer bowel wall as well.

Source: Slide 'Pneumoperitoneum' (standard sign)

Q21High-yield

The upper-central abdominal region between the two hypochondriac regions is called?

Answer C: Epigastric

The 9 regions: R hypochondriac, epigastric, L hypochondriac; R lumbar, umbilical, L lumbar; R iliac, suprapubic, L iliac.

Source: Slide 'Nine regions'
